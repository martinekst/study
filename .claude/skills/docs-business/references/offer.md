# Business content in an offer

Apply the shared `docs-base` offer contract across the portal. In the business
section, make the commercial decision explicit:

- what the customer decides and why now;
- problem, desired outcome, and credible value;
- included scope/options and clear exclusions;
- approach, timing, price basis, assumptions, and dependencies;
- evidence/sample output and the next step.

Do not force one menu across offers. Preserve the existing/reference portal and
place these responsibilities where its readers already expect them. Translate
or explain technical terminology and move implementation detail behind links.

## Map responsibilities before writing

For an existing or reference offer, identify the page that owns each commercial
responsibility and report gaps; do not create duplicate pages merely to match a
template. For a new offer, combine responsibilities into the smallest coherent
top-down sequence:

| Responsibility        | Reader question                                                                   |
| --------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Decision summary      | What decision is requested and why now?                                           |
| Problem and outcome   | What changes for the customer, based on which evidence?                           |
| Value/differentiation | Why this approach and supplier, without unsupported claims?                       |
| Scope/options         | What does each option buy, and what is explicitly excluded?                       |
| Delivery              | What outcomes/milestones, dependencies, and customer inputs shape timing?         |
| Investment            | What price basis, tax/currency, validity, assumptions, and change triggers apply? |
| Evidence              | What sample, case evidence, prototype, or traceable analysis supports confidence? |
| Next step             | What should the reader approve, provide, or schedule?                             |

Variants use a common comparison basis and decision-oriented names. Show shared
scope once, then meaningful differences in outcome, timing, risk, support, and
price. Do not make the lowest-price variant look equivalent by hiding excluded
quality, migration, testing, or operational work.

## Flexible page pattern

```markdown
# <Customer outcome or decision>

<Two or three sentences: customer situation, proposed outcome, requested decision.>

## Why this matters now

<Observed problem/opportunity, consequence, and evidence.>

## Proposed outcome and scope

<What the customer receives; link to detail where needed.>

## Options, investment, and timing

| Option | Outcome/scope difference | Price basis | Timing basis | Best fit when |
| ------ | ------------------------ | ----------: | ------------ | ------------- |

## Assumptions, exclusions, and dependencies

<Visible decision-critical boundaries.>

## Evidence and confidence

<Relevant sample, demonstrator, experience, or analysis with a verifiable source.>

## Recommended next step

<Specific decision/action and what follows.>
```

Keep pricing calculations in the pricing artifact and reconcile every displayed
number to it. A roadmap visual, sample output, or technical appendix is added
only when it materially helps the commercial decision. Mark estimates,
recommendations, and customer-confirmed facts distinctly.
